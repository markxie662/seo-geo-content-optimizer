# The mug with a crooked handle

I nearly threw away the blue mug on Tuesday. The handle had come loose. It wasn't valuable; it was simply the cup I reached for before the room was fully light.

I set it on the windowsill and asked a smaller question: what would I actually miss if I replaced it?

The answer was the weight, the familiar rim, and the memory of a friend handing it to me. A new cup could fill the same place in the cupboard. It would not carry those same things.

This isn't a repair tutorial. I haven't repaired this mug yet, and I don't know whether its materials or damage would allow safe use afterward. I am not offering food-safety advice.

What I can describe is my decision. I will first find someone qualified to assess it. If it cannot return to use as a cup, I may keep it for pencils. I can care about it without insisting it keep the same job.

That leaves me with a few questions to ask before replacing an ordinary object. Do I miss its function, its familiarity, or both? Is there a safe, practical way to keep it? If I need expertise, who can give it?

I do not have to settle every question at once. For this mug, the next step is an assessment. After that, I can decide what keeping it means.
